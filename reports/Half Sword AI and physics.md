# Half Sword's puppet trick, rebuilt for WebXR

Half Sword builds its characters as an **active ragdoll that hangs from an ordinary character capsule**. A hidden, unsimulated skeleton is posed entirely by procedural Two-Bone IK, with no keyframed limb animation. The simulated body chases that pose through joint motors. Balance comes from a free-limit constraint between pelvis and capsule, whose linear and angular motors are retuned continuously ([itch.io, developer comments](https://knives-frank.itch.io/halfsword/comments?before=21)). Mouse movement drives the hand and weapon target, and the developer calls it "really the core of this whole game." Weight emerges because real weapon mass and balance points push against finite "muscle" (motor) strength. Damage combines per-weapon collision shapes, a sharpness flag, location weighting and, since November 2026, rigid plate-armor colliders backed by traces ([Steam: 0.7](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1848664643360622)). The enemy AI is a hand-authored perception-and-reaction layer that the developers rate only "75%" done, and there is no evidence of machine learning. NPCs appear to steer the same physical body the player does. Comparable VR games confirm the core VR recipe. The tracked controller is a target, and a physics hand and weapon chase it through a capped spring. Damage is gated by velocity, edge direction and damager type. Haptic, visual and audio feedback matters more to perceived weight than the physics model does. For a detailed WebXR swordfighter, the lowest-risk stack today is **three.js with WebGLRenderer plus Rapier (WASM)**, with Jolt as the main alternative. Physics runs at a fixed rate with substeps, and each ragdoll's body and joint count is kept small enough to fit the Quest's roughly 11 ms frame budget at 90 Hz. Many details below are inferred rather than published, and each claim is marked as sourced or inferred.

## A hidden IK skeleton pulls a motor-driven ragdoll

Studio director Frank Togonidze has been unusually candid about the character rig. Answering players on the 2022 itch.io build, he described "a hidden skeleton, which is not simulated and plays Anim BP," noted that "all the animations are done procedurally here (so no keyframe animations for arms/legs)," and said he uses "exclusively Two Bone IK for every limb" ([itch.io](https://knives-frank.itch.io/halfsword/comments?before=21)). The physical mesh follows that ghost through joint and constraint motors. His one concrete piece of tuning advice is: **"if you work with joint/constraint motors, I do not recommend using SLERP"** ([itch.io](https://knives-frank.itch.io/halfsword/comments?before=21)). The obvious reading is that he drives swing and twist axes separately. That is an inference, because the actual drive mode, gains and physics-asset layout have never been published.

Balance is the clever part. **"I connect pelvis of the mesh to the collision capsule by a physics constraint… set all the limits to free and use linear and angular motor to keep the body in place and upright… I constantly change the values of those motors powers so that result looks natural."** The result is a capsule and mesh "kind of 'rubber bound' to each other" ([itch.io](https://knives-frank.itch.io/halfsword/comments?before=13)). Walking is procedural too. Before each step the game traces the ground where the foot will land ([itch.io](https://knives-frank.itch.io/halfsword/comments?before=21)). Half Sword therefore never has to solve true centre-of-mass balance. A conventional character controller handles locomotion and ground contact, and the ragdoll hangs off it through a soft six-degree-of-freedom spring. A knockdown happens whenever external forces overwhelm that spring. This mechanism is an inference from the developer's description, since no knockdown threshold or state logic has been documented.

The engine is Unreal Engine 5 ([Steam FAQ](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2397300/discussions/0/4513255283370048156/)). Modders report version 5.4 ([Nexus mod 503](https://www.nexusmods.com/halfsword/mods/503)), which ships only Chaos physics, so Chaos is almost certainly the backend. No source confirms it. The rig was expensive. Update 0.6.1 (June 2026) brought an "Active Ragdoll Total Overhaul… I made this system years ago and it worked well, but was too performance-heavy. **Now it is twice as good performance-wise**" ([Steam: 0.6.1](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1835871199308915)). Its new internals were not described. Hotfix 4 lowered "character physics substepping intervals" for "better physics precision." It also disabled collision between characters and their own weapons, because long weapons were tipping their wielders over ([Steam: Hotfix 4](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1823825466500308)). A later fix for "excessive hips rotation on low framerate" ([Steam: 0.6.1.3 Hotfix 1](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1836506165555048)) suggests that some motor targets were fed per rendered frame rather than per physics step. That is a classic active-ragdoll bug, and a browser build must avoid it from day one.

## Mouse deltas move a target, and mass does the rest

"Yes, mouse movement is really the core of this whole game. Dismemberment and everything else comes later" ([itch.io](https://knives-frank.itch.io/halfsword/comments?before=21)). The developers have not published the exact mapping: plane of motion, depth control and sensitivity curves are all unknown. The structure is clear from patch notes, though. Mouse buttons and modifiers switch between control modes that map the same mouse motion onto different hand targets and grips. Holding the Swap Hands key gives reverse and mordhau grips. Alt-thrust toggles Classic Thrust or Thumb Grip ([Steam: 0.6.1.1](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1835871199310386)). Holding RMB and LMB together gives the half-sword grip ([Steam: Hotfix 1 for 0.6.1.4](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1836506165562235)). Alt alone gives whole-upper-body control, and MB3 gives an "attack chain" mode, described as "a neutral gear for your arms" ([Steam: 0.7](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1848664643360622)). The developers admit that "switching grips is one of the most complicated things you can do with physics based characters and weapons" (same source). Mid-simulation changes to attachment frames are a known source of instability.

Weight is not a damage multiplier or an animation speed. **"All the weapons have real weight and point of balance,"** and when asked to make weights more distinct, the developer answered, "I need to change muscle power of the character" ([itch.io](https://knives-frank.itch.io/halfsword/comments?before=13)). Felt weight is the ratio of weapon inertia to motor strength, and the developers have tuned every term of it. Wrists were made "more rigid so that your weapons flop less." Armor weight now slows movement, and higher strength offsets it ([Steam: 0.6.1](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1835871199308915)). Strength is a persistent, uncapped "muscle" stat that grows with stamina spent ([Steam: Hotfix 3 for 0.7](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1849458702695002)). In 0.7 a player-facing **Weapon Weight slider** arrived, and weapons were made heavier ([Steam: 0.7](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1848664643360622)). A hotfix days later made them "lighter by default" and halved the slider's effect ([Steam: Hotfix 1 for 0.7](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1849192144182511)). Even a team with years on one rig cannot pick a single correct weight. Blade & Sorcery reached the same conclusion, as the next sections show.

## Damage blends physical collision, traces and hit-location weighting

The developers have published no formula. The confirmed pieces fit together as follows. Every weapon and armor piece has "unique collisions that represents its form and ability to do damage" ([Steam FAQ](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2397300/discussions/0/4513255283370048156/)). Cutting depends on a per-weapon **sharpness** state. A 0.7 bug in which "blade making contact with non-cuttable surfaces disables sharpness" ([Steam: Hotfix 3 for 0.7](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1849458702695002)) shows that this state changes during a swing. Damage is weighted by location ("more damage to vital organs, less to non vital"), and there is a dedicated liver hit ([Steam: New Demo Update](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1800357164401182); [Steam: Hotfix 2](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1823191198607822)). Armor protection depends on the metal: "Better metal gives more protection and restricts your movements less," and the "math for armor protection" was refined so that fully armored knights are harder to one-shot ([Steam: 0.6.1](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1835871199308915)).

The most instructive change is the hybrid armor model in 0.7. **"Plate armor now has its own physics colliders attached to characters's body. Before all the collisions were processed through traces… now weapons will physically collide with plate armor and only then the trace will check the rest."** Players can now hook and touch armor plates. The colliders are deliberately "not 1:1" with the visual armor, which bends while the colliders stay rigid ([Steam: 0.7](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1848664643360622)). Rigid plates deflect blades physically, and cheaper traces resolve flesh and soft-armor hits. Blunt damage against armor has been retuned in both directions over two years, with nerfs in November 2024, May 2025 and July 2026 ([Steam: Blood update](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1783872412026800); [Steam: 0.6.1.5](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1837955055363781)). Repeated "explosive damage" bugs from grabbing an arm or body-slamming ([Steam: Hotfix 1 for 0.6.1.4](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1836506165562235)) strongly suggest that damage reads raw contact impulse. Constraint fights between grabbed bodies then produce spurious spikes. That is an inference, but it is a warning any impulse-based damage model must design around.

The injury layer is deep. It includes dynamic mesh slicing ([Steam: Blood update](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1783872412026800)); joint dislocation and bone snapping "by pulling and twisting," with a Bone Fragility slider ([Steam: 0.6.1.3](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1836506165554465)); strangulation leading to unconsciousness ([Steam: 0.6.1.1](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1835871199310386)); and persistent, healable wounds with metabolism and temperature ([Steam: 0.7](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1848664643360622)). Bugs such as "breaking limbs on picking up objects" ([Steam: 0.6.1.3 Hotfix 1](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1836506165555048)) point to bones implemented as constraint-stress thresholds. A trainer mod's HUD tracks per-part health and a "tonus" stat ([HalfSwordTrainerMod](https://github.com/massclown/HalfSwordTrainerMod)). That suggests, without confirming, a global muscle-tone scalar that turns injury into limpness. The cost of all this has been instability. **Permadeath was removed "due to the number of complaints about dying not because of your skill, but because of physics bugs"** ([Steam: 0.7](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1848664643360622)). Early Access reviewers complained that characters ragdolled too easily compared with the demo ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/with-steam-reviews-at-mixed-the-developer-of-half-sword-addresses-differences-between-the-early-access-game-and-its-hugely-popular-tech-demo-the-final-version-of-the-game-will-differ-from-what-has-been-shown-previously/)).

## The AI senses weapons, while physics executes and reacts

No source suggests learned policies. The AI is a hand-built decision layer that has been rebuilt four times in five months. The 0.7 description is the most specific: **"NPCs are much more aware now and will react to combat situations based on their senses. They see threats in front of them, they know where their weapons are, where opponents's weapons are and try to act accordingly. They still make mistakes (especially on lower ranks)… Group coordination is still being worked on"** ([Steam: 0.7](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1848664643360622)). A separate "Social Behavior" system, rated 25% done, adds emotions, personalities, and vision and hearing that depend on light. Golden armor in direct sunlight is noticed more easily than someone in black lying in a dark corner (same source). Earlier patches show a set of tuned heuristics. Less-equipped NPCs are "much less likely to rush a better equipped opponent" ([Steam: 0.6.1.1](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1835871199310386)). NPCs "will parry, riposte, sidestep and back step more often" ([Steam: 0.6.1.3](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1836506165554465)). And "pain makes AI give up, so breaking limbs will end the fight for them" ([Steam: 0.6.1.4](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1836506165560470)).

The dividing line is consistent. **Physics produces the reaction, and the AI reads the resulting body state.** Balance loss, staggering and disarming come out of the simulation, while explicit rules handle surrender, ground-fighting and fighting back when choked. Because the procedural rig has no clips, an NPC attack is almost certainly a hand-target path toward a body part that the motors then carry out. A 2023 player report of a "pre-swing pose" followed by an instant "arm snap" ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2397300/discussions/0/4038101970213132152/)) fits that model of moving to a windup target and then setting a strike target. It is still inference. Player reports also show where this approach fails. Armored AI halfswords and "run[s] at me at mach 8," reacts little to changes of guard, and can be beaten by attacks to the legs ([Steam thread, July 2026](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2397300/discussions/0/590684692649472658/)). Before 0.7, fights often came down to "hitting first." On the other hand, a blade simply held in line toward the player's face acted as an effective passive guard ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2397300/discussions/0/4038101970213132152/)). That last observation is the strongest argument for physically embodied AI: **a guard the AI never chose still blocks.** NPCs also walked into the Crypts' pit until a hotfix ([Steam: Hotfix 1 for 0.7](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1849192144182511)).

Research shows the learned alternative and its price. Won, Gopinath and Hodgins trained physically simulated fencers and boxers whose action is a target posture that a stable PD controller tracks. Emergent aggressive-versus-defensive tactics took **about 1 day of imitation pretraining plus 2–3 days of self-play on 12 two-CPU machines**, and the authors concede that grappling is unsolved ([Won et al., SIGGRAPH 2021](https://hbryu.github.io/Control%20Strategies%20for%20Physically%20Simulated%20Characters%20Performing%20Two-player%20Competitive%20Sports.pdf)). NVIDIA's ASE sword-and-shield character needed roughly a decade of simulated experience ([NVIDIA ASE](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/ASE)). Shipped games take the cheaper route. Blade & Sorcery configures creatures with JSON "Brains" holding parameters such as aim precision and attack speed ([BasSDK](https://kaidevvy.github.io/BasSDK/Modding/SDK/JSON/JSONs-An-Overview.html)). Bonelab's NPCs track locomotion and animation targets with joints that yield during hits ([Thunderstore NPC Physics Overhaul](https://thunderstore.io/c/bonelab/p/DrPIE/NPCPHYSICSOVERHAUL/)).

## VR physics-melee games converge on lagging hands and gated damage

VR removes Half Sword's mouse abstraction. The player's real hand is the target, and the game cannot push it back. Shipped VR titles answer this the same way. **Blade & Sorcery deliberately keeps weapons from following the controller 1:1** to solve the "impact issue," with separate, player-tunable springs and dampers for position and rotation because perceived weight is subjective ([Steam, B&S developer reply](https://steamcommunity.com/app/629730/discussions/2/3362406825551877390)). Unity's XR toolkit formalises the same idea as "Velocity Tracking" ([Unity Learn](https://learn.unity.com/tutorial/grabbable-objects)). Boneworks pushes it to a fully simulated 82 kg body and holds to one rule: **"sticking with a pure simulation where there isn't something introducing an infinite force"**. It admits that this "takes more code and more time" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-classical-game-mechanics-and-physics-converge-in-vr-hit-i-boneworks-i-)). B-Reel's comparison of seven holding methods found that "most of the primary differences weren't even based on the physics." Haptic and visual feedback dominated perceived weight ([Road to VR](https://www.roadtovr.com/b-reel-simulating-object-weight-mass-virtual-reality-motion-controllers/2)). GORN shows the same idea from another angle, making weapons visibly wobbly so that weight reads on screen ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.eu.playstation.com/2020/05/05/over-the-top-gladiatorial-sim-gorn-bashes-onto-ps-vr-later-this-month/)).

Blade & Sorcery's SDK is the best public reference for damage. Each weapon carries per-collider **Damager** components. **Pierce** acts along the blade axis and uses only penetration depth. **Slash** spans the blade's length and cuts forward and backward. **Blunt** works in all directions. A dagger can combine all three ([BasSDK: Damager](https://kaidevvy.github.io/BasSDK/Components/ThunderRoad/Damager.html)). Dismemberment is gated by a `dismembermentMinVelocity` threshold ([Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/bladeandsorcery/mods/6306)), and players learn that slices fail if they lead with the flat ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/629730/discussions/0/3475107714835574158)). Every studio has had to design against waggle. Saints & Sinners uses a stamina meter ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-around-panic-in-i-the-walking-dead-saints-sinners-i-)). Broken Edge "nullifies hyper-speed movements" and stylises blade clashes with an energy backlash, because the game cannot push a real body. It also found latency fatal to physical parries in PvP ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/honing-the-virtual-reality-swordplay-of-pvp-dueller-broken-edge)). Blade & Sorcery shelved early PvP because "latency was too high to be able to parry an opponent well" ([UploadVR](https://uploadvr.com/blade-and-sorcery-interview/)). Until You Fall abandoned free physics for choreography altogether ([Schell Games](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/until-you-fall)). Enemy hit reactions follow the PuppetMaster pattern. Hits reduce pin and muscle weights on the struck chain, while pelvis and spine stay pinned for a stagger, and the body unpins fully only for a knockdown ([Unity forum, PuppetMaster thread](https://discussions.unity.com/t/puppetmaster-advanced-character-physics-tool-released/599766?page=110)).

| Design question | Half Sword (desktop) | VR consensus |
|---|---|---|
| Weapon driver | Mouse moves IK hand target; motors chase it | Controller pose is the target; a PD- or velocity-driven hand and weapon chase it |
| Weight source | Real mass vs "muscle" motor strength; Weapon Weight slider | Spring/damper gap (B&S, player-tunable); feedback dominates (B-Reel) |
| Damage gating | Collider shapes, sharpness flag, location, armor metal | Damager types (pierce/slash/blunt), direction, minimum velocity |
| Anti-flail | Motor strength, stamina-linked muscle | Physics lag, stamina, hyper-speed nullification, choreography |
| Multiplayer | Split-screen only ([Steam FAQ](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2397300/discussions/0/4513255283370048156/)) | Physical parries break under latency (B&S, Broken Edge) |

## Three.js plus Rapier is the low-risk WebXR stack

For rendering, three.js r187 (October 2026) is current. WebXR on `WebGPURenderer` landed in r185 ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-2026-what-changed)), but Meta's own Quest Browser notes still label WebGPU-in-WebXR features experimental. As recently as Browser 152.1 (October 2026), WebGPU composition layers sit behind the "webxr experiments" flag ([Meta Browser 152.1](https://developers.meta.com/vr/downloads/package/browser/152.1/)). The shipping path is therefore **WebGL2 `WebGLRenderer`**. Babylon.js is the integrated alternative, with first-party Havok ([Babylon 6.0](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/announcing-babylon-js-6-0/40044)). Havok, however, has a record of motor bugs: a position motor that doesn't reach its target angle, and a broken custom-axis mode confirmed by a maintainer ([Babylon forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/position-motor-does-not-reach-target-angle/61388); [Babylon forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/physics-motor-joints-with-position-mode-cant-use-a-custom-rotation-axis/62657)). Babylon's ragdoll helper was also reported "not really usable" ([Babylon forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/issues-with-ragdolls-physics-v2/49090)). Those are exactly the features an active ragdoll depends on.

For physics, **Rapier** offers PD motors on spherical, revolute and prismatic joints through `configureMotorPosition(target, stiffness, damping)` with a configurable maximum impulse. It also offers reduced-coordinate multibody joints ([Rapier JS joints](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/joints)). It provides per-body CCD and cheaper "soft CCD", kinematic position-based bodies that derive velocity from target poses, and per-body extra solver iterations ([Rapier JS rigid bodies](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/rigid_bodies)). Its JS bindings now live in the main Rapier repository ([GitHub](https://github.com/dimforge/rapier.js)). **Jolt** is the strongest alternative. Its JoltPhysics.js demos include a "Powered Rig", a ragdoll driven by motors that plays an animation, which is the closest off-the-shelf proof of the Half Sword technique in a browser. Jolt also ships multithreaded and cross-platform-deterministic WASM builds, at the cost of manual memory management ([JoltPhysics.js](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics.js); [demos](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics.js/)). Engine benchmarks are anecdotal and contradict each other ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/which-physics-engine-is-prefered-in-this-case/73956)), so neither engine has a proven performance lead on Quest.

The platform sets hard constraints. Quest 2 defaults to 90 FPS ([Meta perf workflow](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/web/webxr-perf-workflow/)), which leaves **about 11.1 ms per frame** for JavaScript, physics and render submission. Meta advises staggering non-critical updates across frames and keeping to one directional light, KTX2 textures and little overdraw ([Meta perf best practices](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/web/webxr-perf-bp/)). Controller haptics run through `GamepadHapticActuator.pulse()`, which Quest Browser supports and which is widely used ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GamepadHapticActuator/pulse)). Fixed foveation can be set at runtime ([Meta FFR](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/web/webxr-ffr/)). No complete open-source browser VR swordfighter with active-ragdoll enemies exists. The nearest relative is Knight's Waltz, a top-down three.js and 2D Rapier duel. Its swords have mass and inertia, damage comes from relative velocity at the blade contact point, stamina limits spinning, and severed limbs are re-parented to new rigid bodies. Its author reports that "sword feel" tuning took far more iteration than rendering ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/knights-waltz-a-top-down-physics-sword-dueling-game-three-js-rapier-no-game-engine/93523)). Unknowns remain. Quest Browser's support for `SharedArrayBuffer` and cross-origin isolation, which multithreaded WASM requires, was not confirmed. Neither were `XRPose.linearVelocity` support or any measured worker latency.

## Recommended architecture for the WebXR swordfighter

This section keeps two things apart. The **sourced basis** column records what a cited source establishes. The **recommendation** column is engineering judgment derived from it. Nothing in the recommendation column has been measured on a Quest.

### System-by-system direction

|---|---|---|

Revisit WebGPU when Meta drops "experimental". |
| Physics engine | Rapier has PD motors, multibody joints and CCD ([Rapier](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/joints)); Jolt has the Powered Rig demo and multithreaded builds ([Jolt](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics.js/)); Havok motors are buggy ([Babylon forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/position-motor-does-not-reach-target-angle/61388)) | Rapier as the default. | Player weapon | B&S non-1:1 springs ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/629730/discussions/2/3362406825551877390)); Boneworks "no infinite force" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-classical-game-mechanics-and-physics-converge-in-vr-hit-i-boneworks-i-)); Rapier kinematic bodies infer velocity ([Rapier](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/rigid_bodies)) | Kinematic "controller target" body, then a capped PD joint, then a dynamic hand, then a stiff grip joint, then a dynamic sword with CCD and capsule or convex blade colliders. ### Simulation loop and threading

The sourced facts are these. Quest has an 11.1 ms frame at 90 Hz. Rapier's kinematic bodies take a "next" pose each step. Multithreaded WASM needs `SharedArrayBuffer` and cross-origin isolation ([JoltPhysics.js](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics.js)). Half Sword improved its feel by shortening substep intervals and hit framerate-dependent bugs when it did not. The recommendation follows from those facts. Start with physics on the main thread, stepped inside the XR `requestAnimationFrame` callback using that frame's predicted `getPose()`. That avoids the extra frame of controller latency a worker adds. Run a fixed 90 Hz step with 2–4 substeps. Every motor target, IK solve and AI intent must update per substep, never per rendered frame. Aim for roughly 3–4 ms of physics. Move physics to a worker with a shared Float32 pose buffer only if profiling shows the main thread cannot hold the budget, and only after confirming that Quest Browser grants cross-origin isolation. Disable self-collision between each character and its own weapon from the start, as Half Sword eventually did.

### Build order

Each step should be validated on a headset before the next begins. First, the player's sword alone against static targets and a hanging dummy, since controller feel is the core, much as the mouse was for Half Sword. Second, one powered enemy ragdoll that stands, steps and staggers. Third, damagers and armor plates. Fourth, the utility AI driving the shared intent API. Last, gore and persistent injury, which were Half Sword's largest sources of crashes and performance cost ([Steam: New Demo Update](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1800357164401182); [Steam: Hotfix 6](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2397300/view/1825093633183147)).

## Conclusion

Half Sword's important insight is not physics realism. It is the separation of concerns. A cheap, conventional capsule owns locomotion. An IK ghost owns intent. Motors own execution, and their strength becomes a single dial that expresses weight, fatigue, injury, unconsciousness and skill. The AI then only has to choose intents and read body state, because physics supplies blocks, staggers and falls for free. VR makes that architecture both easier and harder. The player's hand replaces the IK target for free, but "one-way physics" means every refusal the world makes must be shown through the weapon, through haptics or through a ghost hand, because it cannot be shown through the player's arm.

The record also sets expectations. A ten-person team on Unreal took years, two rig rebuilds, repeated retunes of weapon weight and blunt damage, and a temporary retreat from permadeath to keep this kind of system fair and stable. A browser build has a fraction of that CPU headroom. The biggest risks for the WebXR project are therefore not rendering or engine choice. They are the ragdoll and contact budget on a mobile chip, and the long tail of impulse spikes from grabs, grip changes and stuck blades. The plan should be built around measuring and clamping both from the first prototype.
