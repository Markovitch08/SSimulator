# Design and Technical Lessons from VR and Physics-Based Melee Games

Scope: Blade & Sorcery, Boneworks/Bonelab, Hellsplit Arena, GORN, Until You Fall, Broken Edge, The Walking Dead: Saints & Sinners, Exanima, and active-ragdoll tech (Unity ConfigurableJoint, PuppetMaster). Researched November 2026. Primary developer sources on the internals turned out to be scarce: most public material is interviews, modding SDK docs and community posts, and none of it is deep. Inferences that rest on no source are labeled as inferences.

## Q1: Standard implementation patterns for physics hands (joint drives or velocity tracking to the controller pose)

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern is a two-body setup. The tracked controller is a kinematic target, and a simulated rigidbody hand or held weapon chases it, either by setting its velocity each physics step or through a spring-damper joint (ConfigurableJoint position and rotation drives). The gap between the two is what produces "weight", collision with walls and blocks, and the "no physical resistance" mismatch. Blade & Sorcery exposes the spring and damper values to players, and its developers say the gap is intentional.

### Cited Findings
- Blade & Sorcery: weapons are deliberately not 1:1 with the controller, in order to solve the "impact issue". Real and virtual hands drift apart by design, and there is a separate spring and damper for position and for rotation that players can tune because perceived weight is subjective — [Steam discussion with developer reply (B&S)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/629730/discussions/2/3362406825551877390), via search summary.
- In B&S, the hand and forearm "flexibility" settings map to joint constraints at the wrist and the elbow, and "spring" is the joint's strength in resisting other collisions. Heavy weapons accelerate slowly and are hard to stop or redirect once moving — [Steam community (B&S)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/629730/discussions/0/1743355067135550800); [Steam community (B&S)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/629730/discussions/1/1814296273133353033). These are player and modder descriptions, not official documentation.
- Some players find B&S weight exaggerated and greatswords awkward. This shows the tuning trade-off — [Steam community (B&S)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/629730/discussions/0/2154350647523285380/?l=english).
- Unity XR Interaction Toolkit's "Velocity Tracking" movement type moves a grabbed Rigidbody toward the interactor using calculated velocities and forces, so it collides with the world instead of teleporting — [Unity Learn: Grabbable Objects](https://learn.unity.com/tutorial/grabbable-objects).
- Common forum recipe: compute velocity = (targetPos − currentPos) / dt and set the Rigidbody's velocity, so the solver integrates the motion rather than teleporting the body. On release, copy the hand's point velocity and angular velocity to the thrown object — [Unity forum, setting centre of mass of FixedJoints / hand velocity](https://discussions.unity.com/t/setting-the-center-of-mass-of-fixedjoints/767545).
- Meta recommends estimating release velocity from several frames of position and velocity history, averaged to smooth out tracking noise — [Meta: Object Interaction Part 3: Releasing Objects](https://developers.meta.com/vr/blog/object-interaction-part-3-releasing-objects/).
- Godot developers report that fast hand motion degrades physics decisions when bodies are teleported, which argues for velocity-driven bodies — [Godot forum: Improving hand physics in VR](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/improving-hand-physics-in-vr/94661).
- B-Reel compared seven ways of holding objects: (1) parenting, which clips through static geometry and has no weight; (2–4) a fixed joint that drops the object when the controller exceeds a speed threshold scaled by mass, with haptic, visual or combined warnings; (5) force pull; (6) a velocity pull that breaks under too much tension; (7) a velocity pull that never breaks. Their finding: "Most of the primary differences weren't even based on the physics", because the secondary visual and haptic feedback dominated perceived weight — [Road to VR / B-Reel: Exploring Methods for Conveying Object Weight](https://www.roadtovr.com/b-reel-simulating-object-weight-mass-virtual-reality-motion-controllers/2).
- Boneworks: a fully simulated body driven by IK from head and hand positions. The player has a fixed mass of 82 kg that sets jump height and the force applied to objects. "If it looks heavy, it is." Two-handed weapons need two hands to stay accurate — [Game Developer: Boneworks](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-classical-game-mechanics-and-physics-converge-in-vr-hit-i-boneworks-i-); [GamesBeat: How Boneworks turns your body into its key mechanic](https://gamesbeat.com/how-boneworks-turns-your-body-into-its-key-mechanic/); [Road to VR: Boneworks reveal](https://roadtovr.com/boneworks-reveal-stress-level-zero-physics-based-interactions-embodiment/).
- Boneworks lesson: "We were able to do it by sticking with a pure simulation where there isn't something introducing an infinite force". The cost is that "it takes more code and more time to implement" — [Game Developer: Boneworks](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-classical-game-mechanics-and-physics-converge-in-vr-hit-i-boneworks-i-).
- Saints & Sinners (Skydance): hands have almost no authored animation. Motion is simulated from player input and only the fingers are animated. Object weight and balance are conveyed through the physics system for held objects — [80.lv: Technical challenges behind Saints & Sinners](https://80.lv/articles/technical-challenges-behind-walking-dead-saints-sinners). Skydance also coined "visual haptics", meaning physics and animation techniques that keep hand motion predictable, believable and effective — [Unreal Engine tech blog: Saints & Sinners](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/skydance-interactive-shows-how-it-injected-vr-physicality-and-melee-combat-in-the-walking-dead-saints-sinners), seen via search summary only (the page returned 403 when fetched).
- GORN made weapons visibly wobbly so players see gravity and forces acting on them, which conveys weight — [PlayStation Blog: GORN](https://blog.eu.playstation.com/2020/05/05/over-the-top-gladiatorial-sim-gorn-bashes-onto-ps-vr-later-this-month/). Its designer called the physics engine "a learning experience" and said "robust" was an overstatement — [Meta blog: GORN on Quest](https://www.meta.com/blog/cartoonish-carnage-gorn-brings-over-the-top-melee-combat-to-the-quest-platform/).
- Unity ConfigurableJoint: Slerp Drive rotates toward Target Rotation using spherical interpolation, taking the minimum total rotation. Position Spring and Damper set drive stiffness. It cannot be combined with X/YZ angular drives — [Unity Manual: ConfigurableJoint](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/class-ConfigurableJoint.html); [Unity: configure driving forces](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/configure-driving-forces.html).

### Inferences
- Recommended baseline for a physics swordfighting game: a velocity- or PD-driven hand rigidbody targeting the controller pose. Grip the weapon with a stiff joint (fixed or ConfigurableJoint) so the weapon's mass and inertia tensor slow the whole hand-and-weapon chain naturally. Expose or tune per-weapon drive strength (the B&S approach). Cap maximum force to stay true to Boneworks' "no infinite force" rule, so a blocked blade actually stops.
- Since B-Reel found that feedback dominates over the physics model, budget tuning time for haptics, visual hand offset cues and audio, not only for joint parameters.
- When the virtual hand lags the real hand, show some cue (a ghost or outline controller, a haptic buzz scaled to the offset). Several titles reportedly do this, but I found no primary source documenting a specific game's ghost-hand implementation (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No GDC talk or postmortem by Stress Level Zero on the Marrow engine or the physics-hand internals was found. GDC Vault was not searched directly.
- No primary source on ghost-hand or offset visualisation in B&S, Bonelab or Hellsplit. This is common knowledge in the community but uncited here.
- The Saints & Sinners Unreal tech blog (the most detailed source on stabbing and weight) returned 403. Only its search-summary content is used.
- No authoritative source on Battle Talent's or Swordsman VR's hand implementation.

## Q2: How damage is tied to velocity, edge alignment and mass

### Takeaway
Physics melee games gate damage on impact velocity, on collision direction relative to the blade's local axes, and on which collider region hits (tip, edge, blunt). Blade & Sorcery formalises this with per-collider "Damager" components of type Pierce, Slash or Blunt. Each has a penetration depth, penetration length and allowed direction, plus velocity thresholds such as `dismembermentMinVelocity`. Anti-waggle is a design layer on top: stamina, nullifying hyper-speed motion, or choreographed combat.

### Cited Findings
- B&S Damager types: **Pierce** uses only Penetration Depth (length 0), direction Forward (along +Z, the blade axis), used for stabbing. **Slash** uses length equal to the blade length and depth from centre to edge, direction Forward and Backward for straight blades, Forward only for axes. **Blunt** has length and depth of 0 and direction All. A weapon can combine several damagers, for example a dagger with a blunt pommel, a slash blade and a pierce tip. Option "Penetration Exit on Max Depth" detaches the weapon at maximum depth — [B&S SDK docs: Damager](https://kaidevvy.github.io/BasSDK/Components/ThunderRoad/Damager.html); [B&S SDK: Items and Weapons](https://kaidevvy.github.io/BasSDK/Modding/SDK/Unity%20SDK/The-SDK-Items-and-Weapons.html).
- For slash damagers to dismember, the SDK says depth must be very small (for example 0.01) — [B&S SDK: Items and Weapons](https://kaidevvy.github.io/BasSDK/Modding/SDK/Unity%20SDK/The-SDK-Items-and-Weapons.html).
- The dismemberment threshold is a velocity value, `dismembermentMinVelocity`, set in the Damager JSON files. Mods raise or lower it to make dismemberment harder or easier — [Nexus Mods B&S mod pages](https://www.nexusmods.com/bladeandsorcery/mods/6306) (via search summary).
- Players report that B&S slices are "hit and miss": you must lead with the edge, not the flat. This is evidence that edge alignment gates damage — [Steam community (B&S)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/629730/discussions/0/3475107714835574158).
- Saints & Sinners stabbing is reported (search summary of the Unreal tech blog) as multi-phase. An enlarged detection volume sits at the tip. Hand motion along the stab axis is treated as force that drives the blade deeper. Embedded weapons need enough pull force to remove — [Unreal Engine tech blog: Saints & Sinners](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/skydance-interactive-shows-how-it-injected-vr-physicality-and-melee-combat-in-the-walking-dead-saints-sinners).
- Saints & Sinners limits swinging with an in-game stamina meter. Boneworks relies on the player's real physical energy — [Game Developer: Designing around panic in Saints & Sinners](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-around-panic-in-i-the-walking-dead-saints-sinners-i-).
- Broken Edge (PvP duelling): the combat system "nullifies hyper-speed movements" such as wiggling, and rewards deliberate, well-planned motion. Touching the body is an instant kill. Deep slices drain the opponent's blade "resources", so players have to protect their own blade — [Game Developer: Honing the swordplay of Broken Edge](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/honing-the-virtual-reality-swordplay-of-pvp-dueller-broken-edge).
- Until You Fall "consciously moved away from more free-form, physics-driven combat" toward a choreographed system that defines the pace and motions of combat while keeping variability — [Schell Games / VRFocus dev diary (Patrick Jalbert)](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/until-you-fall); [Road to VR: Until You Fall gameplay](https://roadtovr.com/until-you-fall-gameplay-video-vr-sword-combat/). GDC talk: "Until You Fall: Building Satisfying VR Combat on a Budget", parts 1 and 2 — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026740/contactUs); [Part 2](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026868/contactUs). The talk content was not retrieved.
- A Game Developer post on VR telekinesis warns that replacing abstracted input with raw physical motion slid into "2006 waggle controls" and felt worse than a button — [Game Developer: Finding the fun in VR-centric design](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/finding-the-fun-in-vr-centric-design-telekinesis).

### Inferences
- A practical damage formula for physics swordplay combines: the relative velocity at the contact point, projected onto the edge normal for slashes and onto the blade axis for thrusts; times the effective mass (weapon mass plus the hand-joint drive contribution); times a damager-type multiplier. Contacts below a minimum velocity, or with the edge more than some angle off the motion direction, do zero damage or only "glance".
- Anti-waggle options seen across titles: (a) the physics lag itself (B&S, where heavy weapons cannot be flicked fast); (b) stamina costs per swing (Saints & Sinners); (c) clamping or nullifying hyper-speed and small-amplitude oscillation (Broken Edge); (d) requiring a minimum swing arc or windup distance; (e) choreography (Until You Fall). Combine (a) with (d) for a sim-leaning design.
- Velocity measured at the weapon rigidbody (lagging) rather than at the controller (instant) naturally penalises flicks of heavy weapons.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives B&S's actual numeric velocity thresholds or the damage formula. Only the field names are documented.
- No developer source found on Hellsplit Arena's damage model. Its listing only claims a "physically correct combat system" — [weloveeverygame listing](https://weloveeverygame.com/games/1039880-hellsplit-arena.html).
- No source on Exanima's damage maths beyond "real momentum, forces and collisions" — [Steam: Exanima](https://store.steampowered.com/app/362490).

## Q3: How enemies react physically (hit reactions, staggering, ragdoll blending)

### Takeaway
The standard technique is an active ragdoll: a physics skeleton whose joints are driven toward an animated pose through joint drives. Hits reduce drive and pin strength (partial or full unpin), then the ragdoll recovers by blending back. PuppetMaster for Unity packages this with global and per-muscle pin weights and muscle weights. Public detail on how specific VR titles tune this is thin.

### Cited Findings
- PuppetMaster's stated scope covers blending between animation and physics, partial ragdolls, and physical interaction with the player character — [RootMotion FAQ](https://rootmotion.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/77000057786-faq); [PuppetMaster GitBook overview](https://assets-1.gitbook.io/assets/puppetmaster).
- Community practice with PuppetMaster: set `pinWeight` to 0 to make a puppet fall. For a stagger, set per-muscle pin weights near 0 but keep the pelvis and spine pinned so the body stays upright, and enable "Angular Pinning" to stop spinning. Keep the character controller capsule on a layer that does not collide with the ragdoll — [Unity forum: PuppetMaster thread p.110](https://discussions.unity.com/t/puppetmaster-advanced-character-physics-tool-released/599766?page=110).
- Classic active ragdoll: each joint is driven toward an animation-derived target. Single-axis joint motors do not cover this well, so Slerp drive or full ConfigurableJoint drives are used — [Unity forum: Joint motor (more than one axis)](https://discussions.unity.com/t/joint-motor-more-than-one-axis/904353); [Unity Manual: ConfigurableJoint](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/class-ConfigurableJoint.html); tutorial repo [brucebarnes/CJ-Test](https://github.com/brucebarnes/CJ-Test).
- Unreal's equivalent (Physical Animation component, pin and unpin) raises the same pin and unpin questions, and users note sparse documentation — [Unreal forum: Physical animation pinning](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/physical-animation-pinning-and-unpinning-the-character/152925).
- B&S added leg sweeping (an idea taken from a mod) and decapitation in updates. Its action set includes grab, punch, kick, parry, throw, stab and smash, which implies physically reactive enemy bodies — [UploadVR: B&S interview](https://uploadvr.com/blade-and-sorcery-interview/); [GamesBeat: B&S interview](https://gamesbeat.com/blade-sorcery-interview-one-developer-redefines-physics-for-vr-melee-combat/).
- Exanima reviewers say "every animation is defined by physics and momentum", and ragdoll can contort limbs unnaturally — [RPGWatch / RPS preview](https://rpgwatch.com/news/sui-generis--exanima--preview-rock-paper-shotgun-31709.html); [Steam: Exanima](https://store.steampowered.com/app/362490).
- Broken Edge avoids simulating blade-on-blade clashes. Blades "break" at the contact point and an energy backlash pushes both players apart, which addresses "one-way physics" (the game cannot push the player's real body) — [Game Developer: Broken Edge](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/honing-the-virtual-reality-swordplay-of-pvp-dueller-broken-edge).

### Inferences
- Hit-reaction recipe: on impact, scale down the muscle strength and pin weight of the hit limb chain by an amount proportional to impulse, and lower the global strength for a stagger. Keep the pelvis and spine pinned unless impulse exceeds a knockdown threshold, then unpin fully. Restore strength over 0.3–1.5 s, and run a get-up animation when the ragdoll comes to rest. This is the standard PuppetMaster-style approach; the specific timings are my estimate, not sourced.
- Enemies' weapons should be held by the same active ragdoll, so a parried NPC weapon gets physically knocked aside. This is the clearest way to get emergent parries for free, but it means AI attacks must survive physical interference.
- Because the player's body cannot be pushed ("one-way physics"), when an enemy blocks the player, the reaction must show up through the virtual weapon (lag, deflection, haptics) or through stylised pushback (Broken Edge).

### Gaps
- No primary source on how B&S, Bonelab or Battle Talent implement NPC active ragdolls, stagger thresholds or get-up logic.
- No primary source on Gang Beasts' active-ragdoll implementation was retrieved.
- Official PuppetMaster per-muscle documentation was not retrieved, so property names come from forum posts and may be version-dependent.

## Q4: Lessons and pitfalls developers cite publicly

### Takeaway
The repeated lessons are these. Tune weight for feel, not realism, and accept the hand/weapon offset that this causes. Feedback (haptics, visuals, audio) matters more than the physics model. Pure simulation without infinite forces prevents exploits but costs development time. Physics engines are fragile. Latency kills physical parries in multiplayer. Waggle and flailing must be designed against explicitly. A credible alternative is to abandon free physics (Until You Fall) or to stylise clashes (Broken Edge).

### Cited Findings
- B&S: realism is deliberately broken (not 1:1) to solve impacts, and weight is player-tunable — [Steam B&S dev reply](https://steamcommunity.com/app/629730/discussions/2/3362406825551877390). Physics melee was "more or less accidentally discovered" and is credited as the game's differentiator — [UploadVR](https://uploadvr.com/blade-and-sorcery-interview/).
- B&S: early PvP was shelved partly because "latency was too high to be able to parry an opponent well". The developer also rebuilt the codebase from scratch (about six months) for flexibility — [UploadVR](https://uploadvr.com/blade-and-sorcery-interview/); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/blade-sorcery-interview-one-developer-redefines-physics-for-vr-melee-combat/).
- Broken Edge: latency acts as a "millisecond fog of war". Clashes are shown as translucent "shadow-collisions" where each client registered the hit, and clash depth is averaged across both clients. Drag-based weight was rejected because it desyncs the real and virtual hands; the game rewards roleplayed deliberate motion instead. Roomscale movement replaced joystick locomotion, and after a clash both players are pushed out of range ("mirror movement") so each playspace stays coherent — [Game Developer: Broken Edge](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/honing-the-virtual-reality-swordplay-of-pvp-dueller-broken-edge).
- Boneworks: "If you feel like you're breaking the puzzles in order to solve them, you're doing it right". Keep puzzles simple, because physics freedom produces unplanned solutions. The game criticised teleportation and the lack of jump, crouch and full-body presence, and restored all three with simulated movement — [Game Developer: Boneworks](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-classical-game-mechanics-and-physics-converge-in-vr-hit-i-boneworks-i-).
- GORN: most development effort went into player feedback, VR design and "wrangling the physics engine". Weapon weight was tuned through early-access feedback — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.eu.playstation.com/2020/05/05/over-the-top-gladiatorial-sim-gorn-bashes-onto-ps-vr-later-this-month/); [Wikipedia: Gorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorn_(video_game)).
- Saints & Sinners: weapons spent a long time in test gyms for tuning. A stamina meter limits swing spam. Throwing items over the shoulder into a pack was added late after watching playtesters — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/technical-challenges-behind-walking-dead-saints-sinners); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-around-panic-in-i-the-walking-dead-saints-sinners-i-).
- Until You Fall: the team chose structured, choreographed combat over free-form physics, and gave each weapon a distinct "feel and weight" without relying on physics — [Shacknews: Jesse Schell](https://shacknews.com/article/110705/jesse-schell-on-making-until-you-falls-weapons-feel-tangible); [Schell Games](https://schellgames.com/portfolio/until-you-fall).
- Players complain when weapon weight is too heavy. One VR melee developer pointed to Hellsplit, Dragonfist and B&S as tuning references — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1910860/discussions/0/3396301606651855468).

### Inferences
- For a single-player physics swordfighter: adopt B&S-style lagging physics weapons (cheap anti-waggle and natural weight), velocity- and edge-gated damagers with per-region damage types, and PuppetMaster-style active ragdoll enemies. Add Saints & Sinners-style stamina as a second anti-flail layer. Avoid multiplayer parrying unless you adopt Broken Edge-style stylised clash resolution.
- Locomotion: offer smooth locomotion plus snap turn and comfort vignettes as options, and favour roomscale footwork for duels (Broken Edge). Physics-body jumping and climbing (Boneworks) increases sickness risk. This is general VR practice, not specifically sourced here.

### Gaps
- Two-handed grip implementation (for example, primary and secondary hand joint priority, or rotation from the vector between the hands) has no primary source in this research. Boneworks only states that two-handed weapons need two hands for accuracy.
- Parry mechanics in physics games (whether a parry is purely emergent collision or gets a "parry window" boost) are undocumented in the sources found. B&S lists "parry" as an action but does not explain it.
- Motion-sickness data or comfort guidelines specific to these titles were not found.
- Battle Talent, Swordsman VR, Hellsplit Arena: no developer postmortems or devlogs were found. Bonelab/Marrow SDK internals were not covered.
